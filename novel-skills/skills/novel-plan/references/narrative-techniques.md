## Story Structure

### Three-Act Structure
- **Act I (25%)**: Setup, character introduction, inciting incident
- **Act II (50%)**: Rising action, complications, midpoint reversal
- **Act III (25%)**: Climax, falling action, resolution

### Scene Structure
- **Goal**: What the character wants in this scene
- **Conflict**: What prevents them from getting it
- **Disaster**: How the scene ends badly or with new complications
- **Reaction**: Character's emotional response and new decision

### Chapter Endings
- End with hooks, questions, or emotional beats
- Avoid resolving all tension within single chapters
- Create forward momentum to next chapter

## Character Development

### Character Arc Principles
- Characters must change or be revealed through story events
- Internal conflict should mirror external conflict
- Character growth happens through difficult choices
- Backstory serves current story, not exposition dumps

### Character Voice
- Each character needs distinct speech patterns
- Dialogue should be identifiable without tags
- Character thoughts reflect their background and personality
- Avoid all characters sounding like the author

### Character Relationships
- Relationships create conflict and drive plot
- Character dynamics should evolve throughout story
- Use subtext in dialogue and interactions
- Show relationship changes through action, not explanation
</conditional-block>

## Pacing Techniques

### Sentence Structure for Pacing
- Short sentences for tension and action
- Longer sentences for reflection and description
- Vary sentence length for natural rhythm
- Use fragments sparingly for emphasis

### Scene Pacing
- Balance action, dialogue, and description
- Accelerate pacing by reducing description
- Slow pacing with introspection and detailed description
- Match pacing to story tension level

### Chapter Pacing
- Alternate high and low tension chapters
- Use white space and paragraph breaks for breathing room
- Control information flow to maintain reader engagement
- End chapters at natural pause points or cliffhangers
